## 1. Scope
- **The spike (G18):** which way Go gets a hexagon grid, judged on four functions: point to cell, cell centre, cells within k rings, and cell outline. The result and the decision are in `docs/engineering/geo/w2-map-grid-and-distribution.md`, decision log rows G7 and G18, and `research.md`.
- **`internal/hexgrid`:** pointy-topped hexagons on a local equirectangular projection around each grid's origin. The Bengaluru grid `blr76` has 76 m edges. The package has no dependencies.

## 2. Spike: what was run
Official vectors from Uber's H3 4.1.2 (Python binding in Docker). There were 3,508 points: 3,000 over Bengaluru, 8 edge cases (poles, antimeridian, equator) and 500 worldwide, at resolutions 9 and 10, which gives 7,016 cells. Each came with its centre, its outline, and for the first 400 points its k=2 disk.

What the tests check:
- **Points:** for 20,000 random points over Bengaluru, each point is inside its cell's outline, nearer its own centre than any neighbour's, and never further than an edge length from its centre.
- **Centres and corners:** every centre resolves to its own cell, and every corner to one of the cells that touch it.
- **Disks:** for k = 0 to 12, exactly 1 + 3k(k+1) cells, no repeats, in ring order, and complete against a brute-force scan.
- **Ids:** they round-trip, and an id from another grid or in a non-canonical form is refused.
- **The real Earth:** neighbour spacing is within 0.12% of √3 × 76 m over the city.

The first version of the nearest-centre test measured on the sphere and failed by 1 cm near an edge, because the projection is about 0.1% off on the real Earth. It now measures in the grid's own metres, where the grid is exact, and the real-Earth error is checked separately.

## 4. Deliberate breaks
Each break was made alone, the tests run, then the code restored (checked with `cmp`).

| Break | Caught by |
|---|---|
| One neighbour offset wrong | `neighbour spacing off by up to 73.320%`; `k=2: {2 -6} twice` |
| Axial rounding without the cube fix | `... in {-191 99} is 91.10 m from its centre but 65.54 m from neighbour {-191 98}` |
| A ring walks five sides, not six | `k=1: 6 cells, want 7` |
| Projection without cos(latitude) | `centre of {-150 -150} is in {-156 -150}` |
| Parse without the reach check | `"blr76:2147483647:-2147483648" accepted` |
| Distance in int32 | `distance 1` |

## 5. Independent review
PASS, with low findings, all fixed:
- Parse accepted far int32 cells, and Distance subtracted in int32. Parse now refuses cells beyond the grid's reach, and Distance works in int. Both have a deliberate break that was caught.
- The projection-error comment held only near Bengaluru. It now states tan(lat) x distance / R.
- w2 step 2 still asked to match the H3 vectors, w3 said "res-10 cell", and G18 should name the real blocker (the Windows dev build). All three are fixed.
- There was no edge-midpoint test. One is added (`TestAPointOnAnEdgeGetsOneOfItsTwoCells`).


## 6. Known limits
- There are no global H3 ids and no parent or child levels. Moving to H3 means rebuilding the cell sets.
- One grid per city. The projection stays within 0.3% out to its 100 km reach, and points beyond that are refused.
- goh3's accuracy is unmeasured. If it becomes usable on all platforms, run the same vectors against it.
