# Test report: relocation rules compared in the fleet simulator (#255)

- Date: 2026-11-14
- Branch: `geo/sim-relocation`
- Environment: Windows 11, Go 1.27.4, local OSRM (scooter profile) on 127.0.0.1:5001
- Simulator only. No API, database or flow change.

## 1. Scope
`sim.RunRelocation` runs the fleet simulator (#214), with riders who can be moved between staging nodes, under three relocation rules on the same orders.

**The rules:**
- **none:** riders stay where they start.
- **today:** `internal/relocation` with `relocation_by_plan` off.
- **plan:** the #226/#249 rule, falling back to today's rule.

**How the simulator runs them:**
- Checks happen every 30 s.
- A moved rider rides the road time between nodes and gets no orders on the way. This is a simplification: in production a rider with a suggested move still gets offers.
- After arriving, the rider rests `relocation_rest_s`.
- Every suggestion is accepted.
- Today's demand is the orders served in `relocation_window_s`, counted at the nearest node.

**The plan:**
- It is fitted by `sim.PlanPicks`: greedy maximum expected covering, with q = 0.2 and capacity 2.
- The candidates are on a 4×5 road-snapped grid.
- Its demand is 200 gates drawn with their own seed (5000), never the evaluated orders.

**The command:** `cmd/sim -fleet N -relocate [-start fixed|plan]`. `-start plan` puts the riders on the plan's first n spots at the start, as assigning spots at check-in would.

## 2. Commands run
`go test ./internal/sim/ ./cmd/sim/` passes.

New tests:
- for today's rule: the window, the minimum, busy and resting riders, a donor with two waiting, and a move in play
- for the plan rule: no orders needed, a donor at its target, the fallback when there is no plan for n, and a move in play
- none never moves
- the same orders for every rule
- a moving rider takes no order
- bad scenarios are refused

The runs are 6 riders, 3 h, 20 seeds, on OSRM. The raw output is in `255-runs-fixed.txt` and `255-runs-plan.txt`.

```
sim -osrm http://127.0.0.1:5001 -zone Z -fleet 6 -relocate [-start plan] -orders-per-hour R -hours 3 -seeds 20 [-hotspots H]
```

## 4. What it shows
1. **Starting at the plan's spots is what helps.** From fixed points, the plan rule mostly gains by moving riders to the plan's spots. Starting them there beats every rule.
2. **Moving riders during the shift, as built, makes things worse.** With good starting spots, the plan rule loses 0.5 to 3.5 points against no moves. It makes about 16 to 29 moves per 3 h, and each one takes a rider out of service while riding. Its plan follows n, the free riders, so it re-spreads every time a rider goes busy or comes back.
3. **Today's rule almost never moves anyone.** With one rider per node there is never a donor with two waiting. It moved riders only where the plan's start put two on one node.
4. **6 riders cover a small share of these 2×3 km test zones** within 80 s (14 to 33% of the plan's demand). The absolute rates show fleet size; they are not the rules' fault.

**Conclusion, as far as these numbers go:**
- Keep `relocation_by_plan` **off**: it does not meet the w4 bar of more orders served at the same or fewer moves.
- Assign spots from the plan when riders start (the owner's attendance idea).
- Before any rule moves riders mid-shift, it needs a gain threshold or hysteresis: move only when coverage improves by a set amount, and not just because a rider went briefly busy. It probably also needs to plan on riders on shift, not riders free at that moment. Each change can be tested here first.

## 5. Known limits
- A moving rider gets no orders here, and gets them in production. That makes the cost of a move higher here than in production.
- Every suggestion is assumed accepted.
- Two test zones, a 4×5 node grid, one demand fit, fixed hotspots, and an 80 s sweet spot.
- Today's rule counts served orders only, as production counts paid orders.

## 6. Deliberate breaks
Each break was made alone and caught, and the file was restored and checked with `cmp`. The breaks are:
- a donor needs two waiting
- the incoming check, in each rule
- rest
- the window
- the plan's donor at its target
- the plan bounds
- the move time
- the rest length
- none never moving

**Four were not caught at first:**
- the incoming check in each rule
- the plan donor
- none falling through to today's rule

Tests were added for each, and all four are now caught. Some first attempts were not real breaks: a perl pattern with `||` and `&&` matched something else. They were redone with literal matching.
